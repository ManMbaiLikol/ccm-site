The listening journey

From social media to the download vendor

Discovery often starts on TikTok and Instagram; it usually ends with a file shared by friends via Xender, or with a song bought for 50 or 100 FCFA from a download vendor.

Bars and other out-of-home venues, along with TV and radio, remain the main channels for new music. To find a song, people use Shazam or ask the DJ.

The four listening routines

5 am – 9 am

Morning, at home

Adults prefer gentle gospel, often as a morning prayer. Teenagers choose loud, danceable music such as mbolé or rap, played alone in their room or while doing chores.

Phone, Bluetooth speaker, TV, apps

10 am – 6 pm

Throughout the day

Most adults do not listen to music, busy with work. A good day ends with a festive song; a bad one, often with no music. Young people move from upbeat to gentler music, then back to loud.

Phone, Bluetooth speaker, TV, radio

7 pm – 10 pm

Evening, at home

When they stay in, listeners choose music that speaks to them personally, away from party music made for dancing.

Phone, Bluetooth speaker, TV

7 pm – late

Evening, going out

In a group, personal tastes take a back seat: at the bar, it is upbeat music at high volume. Later, snack bars and nightclubs take over with better sound.

Bar sound system, live music, DJ decks

What listeners want

The ideal way to listen

Findings from a projective exercise carried out in April 2024 with teenagers, young adults and people in their forties in Yaoundé, Douala, Bafoussam and Maroua.

“I already pay for my data, the app should be free!”

Constant, in his forties, Yaoundé
  • Ideally be free, or avoid multiple payments
  • Let people buy single songs, for around 100 FCFA
  • Confirm each purchase by text message, with the amount and the title
  • Offer a broad catalogue, by genre, artist and country
  • Work on iOS, Android and even basic phones
  • Announce new releases by text message, even offline
  • Show fewer ads
  • Let listeners leave feedback for the songwriters

Digital channels

Platforms used in Cameroon

Boomplay claims 1.5 million Cameroonian users, against 4,700 paid accounts across Spotify, Apple Music and Deezer combined. YouTube and Spotify, the most used platforms in the world, do not yet let Cameroonian artists monetise their streams.
